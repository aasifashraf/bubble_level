Turn your phone into a precise bubble level and spirit level, with ten more measuring tools built in for DIY, carpentry, building and hanging pictures.

Bubble level: lay your phone flat to level shelves, tables and appliances. X and Y show the tilt in each direction and a hint tells you which side is high. When it is level the reading turns orange, with an optional tap and chime.

Spirit level and inclinometer: stand the phone on any edge to read the angle and slope, in degrees or percent. Prefer the classic look? Switch to tube view for a real spirit-level vial. Roofers can read the pitch as x/12.

Angle finder: see how far the phone is tilted, or set a zero anywhere and measure how far it moves from there. Stand it on an edge against a ramp, rail or roof to read the slope of its long and short edges.

Compass: steady magnetic heading even when the phone is tilted, with a warning when nearby metal disturbs it.

Protractor: place an object on the screen and drag two handles along its edges.

Ruler: measure small objects on the screen in mm, cm or inches. Calibrate once with a bank card or a ruler for ±0.5 mm, or just enter your screen size.

Metal detector: find iron, steel and magnets, with a beep that speeds up as you get closer.

Vibration meter: measure vibration on machines and surfaces, with peak, average and a live graph.

Sound meter: measure noise in decibels, with average, maximum and a warning when it gets loud enough to harm hearing. Sound is measured on your phone and never recorded.

Light meter: measure brightness in lux and foot-candles, and see whether the light suits reading and desk work.

Altimeter: read air pressure and approximate altitude, or measure height climbed from a zero.

Height meter: measure the height of a tree or building and the distance to it. Aim the phone’s top edge at its base, then its top, or type the distance if you know it.

Every tool has a short how-to guide. Works in portrait and landscape, in light and dark mode, in 12 languages. No ads, no account, no internet needed.

Some tools need sensors not every phone has. The app tells you up front, and every other tool still works.
